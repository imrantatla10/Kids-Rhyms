Picture a highway with a bridge over it, and a traffic light at each end. That's a simple interchange.

But to get onto the highway, you turn left across two lanes of cars coming straight at you. Guess wrong, and it's a head-on crash.

So you give left turns their own green arrow. Now everyone waits through three light cycles, and the line backs up over the bridge.

So you try something crazy. [mischievous] You flip everyone to the wrong side of the road.

Right before the bridge, the lanes swap places in a big X. Now you're driving on the left, and the on-ramp is on your left too. No one to cross.

That's a diverging diamond.

But headlights right beside you feel scary, so you build a wall down the middle.

Where the lanes cross, a light lets each side take turns. Two phases instead of three.

Walkers get a safe path down the middle, between the walls.

The first one in America opened in Springfield, Missouri, in 2009. Now there are over two hundred.

And the wrong-side trick actually means fewer crashes.
